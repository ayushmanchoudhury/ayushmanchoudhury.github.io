RavenStack: SaaS Churn & Revenue Analytics

PostgreSQLPythonpandasnumpyopenpyxlExcel

The Problem

SaaS finance and customer success teams need four questions answered consistently every month: Is MRR growing or contracting and what is driving it? Which customers are drifting toward churn before they cancel? Are early cohorts retaining well enough? Where should CS spend its time this week? RavenStack is a fictional B2B SaaS company built to model exactly this workflow on a realistic dataset.

What I Found First

Two data quality bugs that would have made the entire analysis wrong. First: accounts that changed plans generated a new subscription row but left the old one open, giving most accounts seven to ten simultaneously active subscriptions. Total MRR read as $10.2M. Real MRR after fixing: under $1.2M. Second: churned accounts never fully disappeared from monthly snapshots because only one of their subscription rows had an end date, making churn MRR show as $0 every month. After fixing both, the waterfall reconciled to $0 difference across all 24 months.

Approach

  • Python validation pipeline checking schema integrity, FK constraints, null audit, and business logic before any analysis ran
  • Five SQL scripts in PostgreSQL covering monthly MRR waterfall (New, Expansion, Contraction, Churn), Net Revenue Retention, and Gross Revenue Retention across 24 months
  • Composite Customer Health Score (0-100) across four signal dimensions: feature usage, support load, contract history, and engagement recency
  • Telemetry disengagement pipeline classifying 390 active accounts into Red, Orange, Yellow, Green tiers by recency and quarter-over-quarter trend
  • 7-sheet Excel workbook generated programmatically with cohort retention heatmap, conditional formatting, and nine documented formula examples

Key Findings

  • Feature gaps are the number one churn reason at 19% of 600 churn events, ahead of support issues (17.3%) and budget constraints (17.3%)
  • 53 accounts representing $161K in MRR are currently disengaged (60+ days of feature inactivity), flagged for CS prioritization
  • Trailing 24-month average NRR of 89.3% shows contraction and churn slightly outpace expansion
  • 33% of the active account base scored At-Risk or Critical on the health score model
  • 10% of churn events were reactivations, sizing a win-back opportunity at roughly $898K in potential ARR
mrr_waterfall.sql (abbreviated)
WITH monthly_subs AS ( SELECT account_id, DATE_TRUNC('month', start_date) AS cohort_month, mrr_amount, LAG(mrr_amount) OVER ( PARTITION BY account_id ORDER BY start_date ) AS prev_mrr FROM subscriptions WHERE is_active = TRUE ) SELECT cohort_month, SUM(CASE WHEN prev_mrr IS NULL THEN mrr_amount ELSE 0 END) AS new_mrr, SUM(CASE WHEN mrr_amount > prev_mrr THEN mrr_amount - prev_mrr ELSE 0 END) AS expansion_mrr, SUM(CASE WHEN mrr_amount < prev_mrr THEN prev_mrr - mrr_amount ELSE 0 END) AS contraction_mrr FROM monthly_subs GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY 1;

E-Commerce Logistics & Customer Analytics

PostgreSQLExcelTableauRFM Segmentation

The Problem

Olist is a Brazilian e-commerce marketplace with 99,000 orders across 27 states from 2016 to 2018. The business questions: Which regions have the worst delivery performance and why? How does a late delivery actually affect a customer's review score? Which customers are worth keeping and how do you find them? What does payment behavior look like across the country?

Approach

  • Seven SQL scripts covering delivery performance by state, delay bucketing, review score correlation, RFM segmentation, and payment behavior
  • RFM scoring using NTILE window functions across recency, frequency, and monetary dimensions to produce five customer tiers
  • Three Tableau dashboards built for specific stakeholder audiences: COO (executive logistics overview), Director of Logistics (delay and satisfaction), and Marketing Analytics Manager (RFM and revenue)
  • Seven-sheet Excel workbook with SUMIFS, XLOOKUP, and PivotTable-driven KPIs

Key Findings

  • The steepest drop in review scores happens between the 1-3 day late bucket (3.3 stars average) and the 4-7 day bucket (2.1 stars). The cliff is at day four, not day seven
  • Orders delayed 8+ days have a 70% one-star review rate vs. 9% for on-time deliveries, an 8x difference
  • AL, MA, and SE (Northeast) have the highest late rates at 21%, 17%, and 15%, with the lowest average review scores in the dataset
  • North states like AM and RO show low late rates but heavily padded delivery estimates, arriving early relative to the promise
  • 97% of customers in the dataset are one-time buyers, typical for a generalist marketplace
  • Credit card accounts for 78.5% of revenue, with average installment counts of 3.5 reflecting Brazilian parcelamento culture
delivery_delay_buckets.sql
SELECT CASE WHEN delay_days <= 0 THEN 'On Time / Early' WHEN delay_days <= 3 THEN '1-3 Days Late' WHEN delay_days <= 7 THEN '4-7 Days Late' ELSE '8+ Days Late' END AS delay_bucket, COUNT(*) AS order_count, AVG(review_score) AS avg_review, ROUND( SUM(CASE WHEN review_score = 1 THEN 1 ELSE 0 END) * 100.0 / COUNT(*), 1 ) AS one_star_pct FROM order_delays GROUP BY 1 ORDER BY MIN(delay_days);

Healthcare Revenue Cycle Analytics

PostgreSQLExcelTableauopenpyxl

The Problem

Hospital leaders deal with three recurring operational and financial challenges: understanding which departments move patients through efficiently, finding where billing revenue is leaking, and figuring out which patients are likely to no-show before they do. This project models all three with separate SQL scripts and outputs so each stakeholder can go directly to what they need.

Stakeholder Design

  • Chief Medical Officer: executive KPIs and clinical operations summary covering encounter volume and average length of stay by department
  • VP of Revenue Cycle: AR aging across four buckets (0-30, 31-60, 61-90, 90+ days) and net collection rate by payer and department
  • Director of Operations: department utilization gaps and seasonal volume patterns
  • Hospital Finance Manager: the full seven-sheet Excel workbook with formula documentation

Dataset Notes

The core tables (patients, doctors, appointments, treatments, billing) are simulated across 200 encounters and 50 patients. The no-show analysis uses a real Kaggle dataset of 110,527 scheduling records from the Brazilian public health system, providing statistical depth that a 200-row simulated table could not support. The two datasets are not joined since the patient IDs are unrelated.

Key Outputs

  • AR aging report bucketed across four time periods with payer-level breakdown
  • Net collection rate per department identifying where billing recovery is weakest
  • No-show rate analysis across scheduling lead time groups, age bands, and SMS outreach status
  • Seven-sheet Excel workbook with conditional formatting and formula examples generated programmatically via openpyxl

Lander Agent: AI Landing Page Engine

Claude APIPrompt EngineeringDTC CopywritingHTMLReplo

What It Does

Lander Agent is a specialized AI agent inside the Creative Engine that generates complete landing pages for DTC brands. It takes three inputs: a brand brief, a target customer avatar, and a creative angle. It outputs a Replo-compatible HTML file ready to paste into Shopify, with scoped CSS, brand-matched assets, and direct-response copy.

Output Types

  • Advertorials structured as editorial-style personal discovery narratives with a conspiracy or authority frame
  • Listicles with progressive CTAs, comparison tables, and an offer-as-final-item structure
  • All output is Replo-compatible: single root div, scoped CSS, no document-level tags, absolute image URLs

The Framework Behind It

Copy is written against an 11-dimension review rubric covering voice-of-customer authenticity, believability, platform compliance, and conversion structure before it gets turned into HTML. The agent drafts copy in markdown first, checks it against the rubric, then converts to HTML after review. This separation prevents shipping copy that reads well but would not convert.

Design Decisions

  • CSS class prefixes scoped per brand (e.g. ht- for HugTerra) to prevent Replo conflicts
  • All CSS selectors scoped with root ID to avoid bleed into surrounding Shopify theme
  • Google Fonts loaded via @import inside the style block, not via link tags Replo strips
  • Semantic HTML tags replaced with divs for Replo rendering compatibility
